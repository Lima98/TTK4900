

% This thesis investigates how a procedural system for melody generation can be developed from simple musical building blocks, and whether artificial intelligence can function as a useful development tool in such a process. The work combines ideas from procedural content generation, music theory, object-oriented software design, and iterative development. The musical goal is not to imitate large data-driven music models, but to construct melodies from pitches, durations, motivic reuse, harmonic context, and melodic constraints in a way that remains understandable and controllable.
%
% The system was developed through three iterations. The first iteration established a proof of concept for generating notes and rendering them with LilyPond. The second iteration introduced more explicit musical objects such as motifs, bars, and phrases in order to improve internal structure and self-similarity. The third iteration expanded this further into a more complete engine with form plans, harmony plans, voice profiles, soft constraints, explicit rendering workflows, and a first SATB-compatible harmonization path. Artificial intelligence was introduced only in the third iteration, after the problem domain and the weaknesses of the earlier versions had become clearer.
%
% The final result is a procedural music generator that can produce single-line melodies and simple harmonized chorale-style outputs, either from automatically derived tonal harmony or from explicitly given harmonic progressions. The generated music is still heuristic and cannot be evaluated objectively in a complete musical sense, but the final iteration produces results that are substantially more structured, singable, and musically coherent than the earlier versions. The thesis also finds that artificial intelligence can be a useful aid for refactoring, implementation, and exploration, provided that it is used as a tool within an informed development process rather than as a replacement for technical and musical understanding.
